✨ Welcome to Lo-ku (Word-Small)

Speak big ideas with tiny, modular words.

Lo-ku is an oligo-isolating constructed language inspired by Toki Pona. Instead of memorizing thousands of vocabulary words, you combine ~60 crisp, two-letter roots with hyphens to build concepts from scratch.

Example Sentence
"Mi se lo-ka."

Translation: "I see a book."

📐 Structural Rules

Grammar, Word Order & Particle Placement

Lo-ku grammar is built for speed and flexibility. Master these four core patterns to build any sentence—from simple greetings to complex questions and tenses.

1. SVO Order & No Articles

S + V + O

Sentences always follow Subject → Verb → Object. Do not translate English "a", "an", or "the"—they do not exist in Lo-ku.

Mi se lo-ka.

I see [a/the] book ("word-object").

Ha gi wa.

He gives water.

To ke fu-mo.

She wants good food.

2. Reduplication (Plurals & Intensity)

X-X = 2×

Say a word twice separated by a hyphen (Word-Word) to make nouns plural or double the intensity of a modifier.

Noun → Plural
Lu → Lu-lu

"Place" becomes "Places".

Modifier → Doubled
Pu → Pu-pu

"Big" becomes "Enormous / Huge".

People → Group
Pe → Pe-pe

"Person" becomes "People / Crowd".

Small → Tiny
Ku → Ku-ku

"Small" becomes "Microscopic / Tiny".

3. Compound Order Matters!

When linking two roots with a hyphen, the order determines the core concept vs. its property. Flipping the words creates a completely different meaning:

Wa-fa vs Fa-wa

Wa-fa (Water + Fire) = Hot water or Steam.
Fa-wa (Fire + Water) = Liquid fire or Lava!

Lo-ka vs Ka-lo

Lo-ka (Word + Object) = Book (makes natural sense).
Ka-lo (Object + Word) = "Object-word" (unnatural for book).

Ka-ba vs Ai-ba

Ka-ba (Physical + Break) = Physically smash/destroy.
Ai-ba (Mind + Break) = Forget, confuse, or heartbreak!

4. Particle Placement Cheat Sheet

Particles attach with hyphens to modify verbs, nouns, questions, and tenses. Memorize which particles attach BEFORE a root and which attach AFTER:

← Before Particles (Prefix) [Particle]-[Word]
Ka- Physical / Matter marker

Distinguishes physical things from abstract concepts: Ka-fa (physical fire), Ka-ba (physically break).

Ai- Mind / Abstract marker

Turns actions/concepts emotional or mental: Ai-ba (forget, confuse, or heartbreak).

Do- Cause / Make marker

Placed before a root to express causing or creating a state (e.g., Do-mo = improve / make good).

Wo- Ownership / Possession

Marks possession before a pronoun or noun (e.g., Wo-mi = my / mine, Wo-yu = yours).

Sa- Question / If / Uncertain

Forms question words: Sa-do (why), Sa-ti (when), Sa-lu (where). Also used standalone at the start of polite questions: "Sa an li yu?" (Polite "Is this you?" vs casual "An li yu?").

After Particles (Suffix) → [Word]-[Particle]
-No Negation / Opposite

Negates or reverses a verb: Go-no (return / go back), Se-no (don't know).

-Pa Past Tense (like English "-ed")

Attaches after verbs: Go-pa (went / traveled), Le-pa (did).

-Te Future Tense

Marks future action: Le-te (will), Go-te (will go).

-Vu Present Tense

Explicitly marks happening right now: Le-vu (doing right now).

Le-

Special Action Particle ("Do")

Le means "Do" and acts as an active verb-maker in front of nouns/concepts: le-fa means "burning or heating something", while le-pa ("did") and le-te ("will") act as auxiliary tense helpers!

🔢 Base-10 Multiplicative System

Lo-ku Number Builder (0 – 9,999)

Lo-ku uses digits 0–9 plus place-value multipliers So (10), Hu (100), and Tu (1,000) linked with hyphens.
